BRAND: DSDN (Down Syndrome Diagnosis Network)
TYPE: 501(c)(3) Nonprofit | Founded 2014 | Stillwater, MN
MISSION: Connect, support, and provide accurate information to parents — and the medical professionals who serve them — from diagnosis through age three, fostering lifelong connections.
VISION: Every Down syndrome diagnosis delivered in an unbiased, factual, and supportive way, and families quickly find meaningful connections.
TAGLINE: You don't have to do this alone.
TONE: Compassionate, empowering, community-centered, medically responsible, hopeful.
POSITIONING: The largest national organization supporting new and expectant parents with a Down syndrome diagnosis. Recognized by the American Academy of Pediatrics as a preferred resource.

VOICE CHARACTERISTICS:
- Warm, inclusive, uplifting — lead with families, not the organization
- Person-first language always: "person with Down syndrome" or "child with Down syndrome"
- Never use: "suffering from," "afflicted," "affected by," "birth defect," "retarded," "defect," "special needs" (prefer "disability" or "Down syndrome")
- Use: "diagnosis," "extra chromosome," "Trisomy 21"
- Emphasize ability, community, connection, and love
- Compassionate but not pitying: empower, don't patronize
- Medically accurate: evidence-based, AAP-aligned
- Sensitive to the prenatal diagnosis context — many families learn during pregnancy
- Celebrate milestones — big and small — without comparison
- Short paragraphs for emotional readability; bullet points for resources
- Never use em dashes (—) or en dashes (–). Use commas, periods, or " - " (single space before and after) instead.

MESSAGING PILLARS:
1. CONNECTION — "You are not alone" is the foundational message. Speed from diagnosis to community.
2. SUPPORT — Practical, emotional, peer-to-peer. From diagnosis through age 3 and beyond.
3. INFORMATION — Accurate, unbiased, medically reviewed. Partnered with AAP and GLOBAL.
4. COMMUNITY — Retreats, peer groups, mentors, identity-based spaces, celebrations.
5. MEDICAL OUTREACH — Improving how providers deliver diagnoses with compassion and accuracy.

SENSITIVE CONTEXT:
- Many families receive a prenatal diagnosis — messaging must respect all outcomes including loss
- Privacy is paramount — never share family stories without explicit consent
- Avoid "inspiration porn" — celebrating children for simply existing with a disability
- Loss families are part of this community — memorial wind chimes, care cards
- Diversity matters — 30+ identity-based groups, Spanish-language materials, IDEA plan
- Frame challenges as opportunities, not deficits
