At a high level, Optum Financial facilitates payments and money movement across the broader health care landscape. The major constituents are:
- Payers, which are health insurance companies
- Providers, which can be medical, pharmacies, dental providers, really anybody who accepts insurance payments or payments from consumers
- The end consumer, who will often have to pay a provider whatever their insurance company is not paying
- Employers who offer health insurance benefits to their employees, which are the end consumers
I want to break down each of these in more detail.

Optum Financial has a variety of payment capabilities that have been developed and acquired for payers to be able to pay providers. There is a legacy product called Optum Pay that allows United Healthcare and other payers to pay their providers through an ACH payment with straight-through processing. It posts to their account in one to three business days. This is a legacy product that was built by Optum specifically for United Healthcare. There are various options within OptinPay. At a high level, there's a basic version with 6,000 TINs (which are tax ID numbers), and there's a premium version with 114,000 TINs. 

There's another product called Optum vPay that is around payers being able to pay a provider through a virtual card that is instant. The payer gets a kickback of 2-3% on the payments that are made to the provider. It is a major source of growth in revenue for a health insurance payer, but there is legislation that is potentially going to curb growth potential here. 

There's also a couple of products that were purchased from an organization called Change Healthcare and their solution, which is called NPX (National Provider Exchange). There are two components of NPX:
1. A solution called MCDS that facilitates printed checks and communications. It's a platform for checks and explanation of payments from payers to providers.
2. Another platform underneath called NGP, which facilitates image cash letter (ICL) deposit creation. Basically, it converts paper checks into electronic payments.As part of this, it also validates the payment with the health insurance claim that gets posted to the provider's systems, which can sometimes be manual, but this is an automated reconciliation for that. For this to happen, there is a bank that is leveraged to move money from the payer to the provider called CASSBANK, where it takes one large payment from the payer and then breaks it into millions of provider accounts. There are about 150 payers that are using some version of these solutions, and currently 50,000 providers that are set up. There is a 1.49% origination fee that is earned along the way. 

Separately, there is another company called Echo that integrates with many payers and providers. They are larger than what we offer. Their product is called settlement advocate. There's a joint venture between Optum Financial via Change Healthcare and Echo, where our npx solution is embedded inside of Echo and white-labeled for us to be able to sell. This is a major distribution point for us to get access to more payers and providers, but inserting ourselves in the middle.

Separately, Echo also sells to payers and providers without our solution embedded. There's a separate opportunity for us to distribute npx further among the Echo payers that are not using our solution currently, which is about 250 to 300. All of these solutions to facilitate payers paying providers are bespoke solutions that have been built or acquired. There's a major development effort to bring these all into one platform called Connexions or Payments 2.0. Instead of having separate enrollment processes and portals for each of these solutions, which is challenging for payers and providers, this will be brought into one unified enrollment and consolidate seven systems down to one. There's a partner called Zeta that is helping us with this. We've invested into the business to help facilitate this happening. 

Providers sit at the center of the ecosystem. There are 2.8 million providers in Optum's network, and currently $550 billion flows through our payment rails. We're contracted with 80,000 provider TINs that enable us to monetize these payments. There are several offshoots of our provider strategy currently. 

Fiserv is a major player in this space. There are a couple of components of Fiserv:
1. They facilitate consumers being able to pay providers through a platform called Clover Practice Pay.
2. Fiserv enables payments to many different types of businesses.
3. To be able to get to the healthcare-specific transactions and providers, they embed another solution from Rectangle Health and their practice management solution.
Rectangle Health is a merchant processor with 40,000 providers and processes $20 billion in payments.

We are working on a referral agreement for us to refer our providers to use the Clover practice pay solution with Rectangle Health embedded, and we would earn a revenue stream from that. Separately, we are looking at NNA discussions with Rectangle Health to potentially acquire that piece directly, which is embedded inside of the FISERV solution.

Separately with FISERV, we are looking to integrate their Clover practice pay solution into our S3 merchant network. We have a network of merchants (grocery stores, pharmacies, etc.) that are integrated with our directed spend cards. Directed spend cards allow employers or anyone to put money onto a card and be able to dictate where those dollars can be spent at specific merchants, specific product categories like foods or pharmacy, or even at a SKU level like healthy foods or specific brands or dosages of a medication. This closed-loop network allows us to control the entire spend where consumers are spending their money. If we could integrate Clover into this S3 network, it opens up more merchants beyond traditional retail groceries and into a broader ecosystem of merchants that Clover is integrated with. 

Lastly, Fiserv has a major banking platform that facilitates banking transactions for major banks. We've recently converted to a new platform under Fiserv called Finzact. That is the banking platform that we will be using at Optum Bank. At Optum Bank, the traditional business has been around health savings accounts and flexible savings accounts. We will be bringing a lot of these accounts into a singular view called an Optum Wallet that will enable a consumer to have one view of all of their accounts and then be able to spend their money more efficiently at a provider group. 

For Optum Bank's core traditional business of HSAs and FSAs, they have also got into lending recently on the consumer side. They do this through a variety of partners.
A third of the consumer lending is done through a company called Proceed Finance that focuses on lending inside of a dental office. We are acquiring Proceed in the coming months. They have a strong marketing arm for reaching providers that we want to also expand upon for some other efforts.
The other two-thirds of consumer lending is done through three major partners:
1. BHG, which stands for Bankers Health Group. We take about 500 million of their 2 billion loans that we buy from them. They originate them, and we buy the loans from them on a quarterly basis.
2. A company called Cherry that is a fast growth company. They're three times the size of Proceed and also focus on dental. They are the largest originator there of loans, but they also focus on audiology and veterinary. Three billion dollars of total volume, and we buy about a third of that volume on a weekly basis.
3. A company called PatientFi. We are their bank partner. They dominate the med spa and fertility space. We can buy up to 80% of their loans. We do about 60% and sell the remaining 20% on a weekly basis.

There's also another company called Cedar that helps facilitate consumer payments to providers that we are in discussions with around acquisitions. 

Providers are at the center of the strategy and will be a big part of my role in finding partnership and distribution opportunities. We are rolling out a core bank account for providers right now that they can sign up with a bunch of benefits. We have also offered a variety of lending products to providers that allow them to continue to grow their business and help manage some of the cash flow challenges in getting paid from payers and consumers in continuing to grow their business.

We have one lending product in market today called Provider Working Capital, or PWC, that allows a provider to either take a cash flow loan or a capital loan out, and we then get paid back from their future claims that are received from a payer. We open a Stripe virtual bank account that their claims get directed to. We then take our fee or interest before their claim payments hit their other bank account.

We're getting ready to roll out traditional term loan products and a line of credit that would allow a provider to have a p-card or purchasing card that they can buy items they need for their business up to their line of credit, where we make money on the transactions. This is a big area of focus on how we grow the number of providers who have financial relationships with us. Providers can be traditional medical providers, pharmacies, dental offices, or a variety of others.

On the pharmacy side, we own a pharmacy benefit management company called OptumRx that processes many pharmacy claims and rebates in this ecosystem that we are working with internally. We also own a company called OptumInsight that builds technology solutions and helps providers to improve the efficiency of their operations, who we are also working to introduce these offerings to. There's a company called Sencora that we are building out a custom solution called ConvergeRx for small to mid-size independent pharmacies in our OptumInsight business and integrating with point-of-sale terminal solutions from a company called EpiCor, where we can introduce Optum Financial and Optum Bank within their solution. On the dental front, there's a company called Henry Schein where there is a partnership opportunity.
